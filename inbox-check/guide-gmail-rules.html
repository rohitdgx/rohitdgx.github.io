Gmail, Yahoo and Outlook sender rules

The three biggest inbox providers now check the same few things before they accept your email. Here's what applies to you.

Rules for every sender

Even if you send a handful of emails a day, Gmail and Yahoo expect:

  • SPF or DKIM set up for your domain (both is better).
  • Mail sent over a secure (TLS) connection. Normal providers do this already.
  • A spam complaint rate below 0.3%, ideally under 0.1%.
  • Valid DNS for your sending servers. Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 and Zoho handle this for you.

Extra rules for bulk senders

Google counts you as a bulk sender once you send about 5,000 messages a day to personal Gmail addresses. Yahoo uses a similar line, and Microsoft applies its rules to senders above 5,000 a day to Outlook.com and Hotmail. Bulk senders need:

  • Both SPF and DKIM, passing.
  • A DMARC record, at least p=none, aligned with your From address.
  • One-click unsubscribe in marketing emails, and unsubscribes processed within 2 days.
  • Spam complaints under 0.3%.

What changed recently

Google and Yahoo announced these rules for February 2024 and phased them in. In November 2025 Gmail moved from warnings to rejecting non-compliant bulk mail outright. Microsoft started applying the same requirements to high-volume senders to its consumer inboxes in 2025.

Small sender? You may not hit 5,000 a day, but SPF, DKIM and DMARC still decide whether your invoices and replies land in the inbox. Setting them up takes under an hour.

Your checklist

  1. One SPF record that includes your email provider. SPF guide
  2. DKIM switched on at your provider. DKIM guide
  3. A DMARC record, starting at p=none. DMARC guide
  4. An unsubscribe link in every marketing email.
