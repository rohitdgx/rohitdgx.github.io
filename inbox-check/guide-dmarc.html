DMARC: block fakes safely

DMARC tells receivers what to do with email that claims to be from you but fails SPF and DKIM. It also sends you reports showing who is sending as your domain.

Your first DMARC record

v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@yourcompany.com; adkim=r; aspf=r; pct=100

Add it as a TXT record with Name/Host _dmarc. Use an inbox you check for rua; reports arrive as daily zipped files from Google, Microsoft, Yahoo and others.

What each part means

  • p=none: watch only. Nothing is blocked, you just get reports.
  • p=quarantine: emails that fail go to spam.
  • p=reject: emails that fail are refused.
  • rua=: where daily reports go.
  • pct=: what share of failing mail the policy applies to. Keep it at 100 once you're past testing.
  • adkim=r; aspf=r: relaxed alignment, which allows subdomains like mail.yourcompany.com.

The safe rollout

  1. Weeks 1-4: p=none. Read the reports and list every service sending as you (newsletter tool, invoicing, helpdesk).
  2. Add each legitimate service to SPF and switch on DKIM for it.
  3. Next: p=quarantine. Watch for complaints that real emails went to spam.
  4. Finally: p=reject once reports show only your own services passing.

Jumping straight to p=reject is the most common mistake. It can silently block your own newsletters or invoices if one tool wasn't set up yet.

Only one DMARC record

Two TXT records at _dmarc make receivers ignore both. Edit the existing one instead of adding another.
