SPF: say who may send as you

SPF is a single line in your DNS that lists the services allowed to send email for your domain. Receivers check it on every email.

What a good SPF record looks like

v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all

It starts with v=spf1, lists who may send (here, Google Workspace), and ends with ~all, meaning "anything else is suspicious".

The include for your email provider

  • Google Workspace: include:_spf.google.com
  • Microsoft 365: include:spf.protection.outlook.com
  • Zoho Mail: include:zoho.com (India accounts: include:zoho.in, EU: include:zoho.eu)
  • GoDaddy email: include:secureserver.net
  • Hostinger: include:_spf.mail.hostinger.com
  • Titan: include:spf.titan.email

Also add any tool that sends as your domain, such as SendGrid (include:sendgrid.net) or Mailgun (include:mailgun.org). Inbox Check has tick boxes for the common ones.

Three rules that break SPF

  1. Only one SPF record. Two records starting with v=spf1 make both invalid. Merge them into one line.
  2. 10 DNS lookups maximum. Each include, a, mx or redirect costs lookups, and includes inside includes count too. Remove tools you no longer use.
  3. Never end with +all. It lets anyone send as you. Use ~all while you test, -all once you're sure.

How to add it

  1. Open your DNS host (GoDaddy, Cloudflare, Namecheap, Hostinger…) and find DNS records for your domain.
  2. Look for an existing TXT record starting with v=spf1. Edit it, or add one if none exists.
  3. Type: TXT. Name/Host: @. Value: your SPF line.
  4. Save, wait 15-60 minutes, then run Inbox Check again.
