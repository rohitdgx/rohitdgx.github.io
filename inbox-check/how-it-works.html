How Inbox Check works

Every email you send carries your domain's name. Before Gmail or Outlook decides inbox or spam, it looks up a few public records to see if the email is really from you. We read the same records and tell you what they see.

The 4 steps

Step 1

You type a domain

Type yourcompany.com or any email address on it. We clean it up, so https://www.yourcompany.com/contact works too.

Step 2

We look up public DNS records

Your browser asks Google Public DNS (Cloudflare if Google is slow) for the MX, SPF, DKIM and DMARC records, plus MTA-STS and BIMI. These records are public by design; anyone's mail server reads them. Nothing is stored.

Step 3

We score and explain

Each record is checked against the rules Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft publish: is it there, is there only one, does it include your email provider, does the policy actually protect you.

Step 4

You fix it, or we do

Copy the records into your DNS yourself using the report, or order the $9 fix and we walk you through it and re-check after 48 hours.

  • Your SPF ends in ?all, so it protects nothing.
  • Your mail runs on Google Workspace but SPF doesn't include it.
v=spf1 include:sendgrid.net include:_spf.google.com ~all

Email signature (DKIM) 3

Needs a look

No key found at google._domainkey. Switch on DKIM in the Google Admin console.

What to do with fakes (DMARC) 4

Fix this

No DMARC record. Add this at _dmarc:

v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@sunrise-bakery.example; adkim=r; aspf=r; pct=100
1

Provider and score. We read the MX records to see who runs the inbox, so every fix is written for that provider. The score adds up the four checks below.

2

SPF. Two problems here: the record ends in ?all, which tells receivers "no opinion", and it forgets Google Workspace, so the bakery's own emails can fail. The report hands over a corrected record to paste.

3

DKIM. We try the usual key names for the provider. Google uses google. Nothing there means signing is probably off.

4

DMARC. Missing, so Gmail may reject this bakery's newsletters and anyone can fake its address. Start with p=none, watch reports for 2-4 weeks, then tighten.

What it can't tell you

Content and habits

A perfect setup doesn't save spammy content, bought lists, or a 1% complaint rate. Records prove who you are; behaviour decides the rest.

Custom DKIM names

Some tools sign with a key name we can't guess. If DKIM shows "Needs a look" but you know it's on, check your provider's DKIM page.
