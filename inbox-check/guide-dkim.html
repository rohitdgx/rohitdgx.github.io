DKIM: sign every email

DKIM adds a hidden signature to each email. Receivers check it against a public key in your DNS to confirm the email really came from you and wasn't changed.

How it works in one minute

Your email provider holds a private key and signs every message. You publish the matching public key in DNS under a name like google._domainkey.yourcompany.com. The part before ._domainkey is called the selector.

Google Workspace

  1. Open the Google Admin console, then Apps, Google Workspace, Gmail, Authenticate email.
  2. Pick your domain and click Generate new record (2048-bit if your DNS host allows it).
  3. Add the TXT record it shows at google._domainkey in your DNS.
  4. Wait about an hour, then click Start authentication.

Microsoft 365

  1. Open the Microsoft Defender portal, then Email & collaboration, Policies, Email authentication settings, DKIM.
  2. Pick your domain. Microsoft shows two CNAME records, selector1._domainkey and selector2._domainkey.
  3. Add both CNAMEs at your DNS host exactly as shown.
  4. Back in Defender, switch DKIM signing on.

Zoho Mail

  1. Open the Zoho Mail Admin Console, then Domains, your domain, Email configuration, DKIM.
  2. Add a selector (for example zmail) and copy the TXT record.
  3. Add it in your DNS, then click Verify in Zoho.

Other providers

Look for "DKIM", "email authentication" or "domain verification" in your email admin panel. GoDaddy plans built on Microsoft 365 use the Microsoft steps. Hostinger, Proton and Fastmail show their DKIM records on the domain settings page.

If Inbox Check says it couldn't find DKIM but you know it's on, your provider may use a custom selector name. Check the provider's DKIM page to confirm it says "active".
